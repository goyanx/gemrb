# Terralum campaign expansion plan

The eight-act cadence follows the broad chapter structure of *Shadows of Amn*. Terralum's plot and places come from the early Alsperia transcript, the later recovered lore codex, or explicitly proposed adaptation material. The current game implements a short chapter spine. The targets below describe a longer future game; they are not current playtime claims. Dorian's Act III purpose test, Tessa's Act II lantern decision, Ilya's creekside investigation, the Lost Wayfarer's Mistwood privacy decision, and Scholar Inessa's Library route decision are authored as native Infinity Engine dialogue alongside the campaign panel; both interaction routes write the same persistent choice state. Inessa's two branches, changed re-entry lines, and matching Act I Journal events are live-verified in separate GemRB builds. Both outcomes of Mara's campaign-panel and native DLG ledger choices now survive named save/load and change Inessa's later Library response. Archivist Leora's native DLG carries the existing patrol-schedule choice into the recurring Waywarden thread; the visible Archive cast, direct click-to-talk, copy branch, and its re-entry line are live-verified. Saren's later callback remains open.

The current build has twenty-seven playable scene definitions, 68 story beats, 70 named cast placements, and 34 one-time journal events across the eight-act prototype. Act VIII adds the Eldrador Outer Watch Camp as an explicitly labeled adaptation: four named NPCs, a required record-keeping choice, conditional responses, a one-time Journal entry, female Kokoro narration, and a sealed-sanctuary boundary. Its new background, painted Nerys sprite, and character-specific cast were reviewed in isolated GemRB. The required-choice gate, closing beat, and Act VIII Journal entry are live-verified; a named save/reload preserves the selected watchbook policy response and Journal entry. Act III now returns the party to the Mistwood Watchpost after Lorian. Courier Pell asks whether to leave a public pale-stone route marker or keep the complete patrol map in King Edmon’s record; the journaled choice changes both Edmon's and Queen Alisande’s court responses. Edmon remains present as Alsperia's original king and Alisande's husband in this early period. This adaptation revisits the location without assigning new history to the source. Act IV now includes a journaled access policy from Captain Rovan, followed by an adaptation evidence-docket quest: Royal Scribe Oren asks Algoreth whether Dorian's route testimony belongs beside the scroll account or should remain a separate record. Courier Pell then offers an optional deposition, letting Algoreth decide whether the court publishes only the traveller warning or keeps the full route details in the crown file; the branch is journaled and remains limited to Pell's direct knowledge. Both outcomes change Alisande's council response without asserting the scrolls' purpose or filling in the missing reign chronology. Act I includes the Royal Dispatch Archive and Royal Stables; Act II includes the Hunter's Camp, River Crossing, Three-Cairn Fork, the road and watchpost, then the Waywarden Relay before Lorian. On Mistwood Road, an optional Lost Wayfarer side quest lets Algoreth send Courier Pell an anonymous warning about an answering voice or keep the account private and report only the unsafe road; Pell later acknowledges the choice without identifying the voice. The relay choice carries the copied-patrol schedule forward and records whether the party calls the wardens. Act V includes the Academy Containment Annex between the courtyard and vault, where the player chooses a measured boundary or an explicit containment purpose. The Observatory Vault encounter has a Ward Shard phase followed by the named False Algoreth, whose four dialogue states reflect both earlier warding decisions; the seal resolves in the journal only after both fall, with a pre-fight save as the recovery route. Act VI includes Archivist Nera’s optional Index Warden inquiry, Selene’s staged follow-up, and an Archives Trial Hall adaptation based on the recovered Tidewarden Thalor and spectral guardians account: the three guardians present a boundary, truth, and reconciliation clue before Algoreth records one of two principles for the Eldrador expedition. This is a remembered test, not a resolution of either Tear; the hall leaves open whether the Deep Archives and Eternal Archives are the same place. If the account remains sealed, Algoreth chooses a bounded question for Eldrador’s warders. The titleless living record has no recovered speaker identity; the question changes Vaelthira’s welcome but does not answer what the record means. Act VII now follows the supported Aethercraft departure with two adaptation scenes: at Eldrador's Landing Quay, the party chooses whether to record witness accounts or copy measured seal records; at the Lower Sanctuary Threshold, the party chooses a cautious approach to the still-sealed gate. These events are journaled and change later dialogue, while the campaign stops before entering or resolving the Tear because no outcome is present in the recovered lore. Act VIII adds an exterior watch camp where a player choice changes how witness testimony and measurements are kept; it neither identifies the familiar voice nor crosses the seal. All areas still inherit demo WED geometry and character silhouettes. All twenty-seven scenes now have bespoke searchmaps; their WED geometry and terrain resources still inherit the demo layout. Algoreth answers Dorian's purpose question before the guardian fight, and the answer changes the Guardian's response and persists in the journal. The Seal Gallery requires an inscription answer, journals it, and changes Alisande's return-court response.

The intended full campaign could grow to about 25-30 quests and 20-30 distinct areas, with branching dialogue, companion arcs, meaningful combat and a later Eldrador continuation. A 30-45 hour playtime is only a design target. The current twenty-seven-area slice still needs area-specific WED/terrain data, deeper companion arcs, authored quest journals, durable multi-stage dialogue, enemy art and balance, party systems, and save/load regression coverage.

## Lore boundary

The early Edmon–Alisande marriage is a direct user clarification. The later Algoreth–Alisande marriage and co-rule are stated in the recovered codex. Their transition, Edmon's fate, the Eldrador Tear's outcome, and the intelligence behind the Tears are unresolved in the available sources. New quests and dialogue must be marked as adaptation proposals, as described in [`lore_notes.md`](lore_notes.md).
