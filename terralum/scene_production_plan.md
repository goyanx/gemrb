# Terralum scene production plan

## Template drawn from Baldur's Gate II

The binding contributor baseline is [GOLD_STANDARD_GAME_DESIGN_PIPELINE.md](GOLD_STANDARD_GAME_DESIGN_PIPELINE.md). This file tracks delivery slices; the gold-standard file defines acceptance gates.

BioWare's postmortem describes a stable engine and content pipeline that let most development time go to authored content. It gives a repeatable area process: designers map and describe an area, concept artists develop the isometric idea, the level is built and dressed, then designers author clipping, lighting, height, and the search map. Its lessons also call out project scope and quality assurance as core constraints. Interviews with BG2 writers and designers describe dialogue authored with variables, technical designers implementing actor spawns, movement, rewards, and event timing, and quest arcs being outlined in beats before implementation. A BG2 retrospective emphasizes putting quests, items, encounters, and people into explorable spaces, and giving companions distinct motives and relationship arcs.

Sources: [BioWare's BG2 postmortem](https://media.gdcvault.com/GD_Mag_Archives/GDM_March_2001.pdf), [David Gaider on Infinity Engine writing and implementation](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103473/beneath-a-starless-sky-pillars-of-eternity-and-the-infinity-engine-era-of-rpgs?page=15), [Kevin Martens on BG2 area content](https://www.insidemacgames.com/historical/news/story.php%3FArticleID=1621.html), [BG2 retrospective](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/25-years-ago-today-baldurs-gate-2-set-rpgs-on-the-path-to-becoming-the-industry-defining-genre-they-are-today-we-were-putting-all-the-fantasies-that-we-had-into-the-game/).

## Rules for Terralum implementation

1. Keep each area focused on one story purpose and fill it with a named cast, discoverable clues, a quest/event, and a tactical or social encounter.
2. Write an area one-pager and its beat sequence before adding script events. Keep implementation constraints and story choices visible in the same data.
3. Give every recurring NPC a stable identity, role, motivation, voice text, and return beat. Do not turn unresolved lore into asserted history.
4. Make interaction change a durable quest flag, reveal a clue, open a route, or alter an encounter. Avoid dialogue that only relabels the Continue button.
5. Build geometry, walkability, actor placement, and scene art together; the current shared demo walkmesh is a temporary template and must not ship as finished areas.
6. Keep authored content achievable: deliver and playtest one act at a time, then expand only after the area has working pathing, dialogue, events, music, and saves.



## Act II side quest brief — The voice in the trees

**Status:** implemented adaptation; choice, journal, and callback live-tested in isolated GemRB sessions; all four callback state combinations covered by campaign-flow checks. **Area:** Mistwood Road (`tl0002`), with a callback at the Ruined Watchpost (`tl0009`). **Estimated play time:** 2–4 minutes, optional. **Entry:** the player may speak with the stranded Lost Wayfarer before continuing along the road. **Exit:** Tessa and the party proceed with the existing Mistwood route or encounter; this conversation never gates the main quest.

- **Player goal and obstacle:** decide what warning should reach the next travellers. The Wayfarer heard a voice answer their name from the trees but cannot identify its source. The obstacle is a real tradeoff between passing on a useful warning and protecting a shaken traveller's privacy.
- **Beat sequence:** (1) Talk to the Wayfarer and hear the limited account. (2) Choose to send Courier Pell an anonymous warning about the answering voice, or keep the account private and report only the unsafe road. (3) Record one shared journal event under Act II and close the decision so it cannot be overwritten. (4) At the Watchpost, Pell acknowledges the exact reporting choice. If the road ambush was defeated, his line also acknowledges the cleared road and recovered rubbing.
- **State:** `TLWAYFARER=1` means anonymous voice warning; `TLWAYFARER=2` means account private and road hazard only. `TLJRN_WAYFARER_PUBLIC` or `TLJRN_WAYFARER_PRIVATE` records the selected branch; exactly one is written per playthrough. Pell's response checks the reporting choice together with `TLMISTWOODVICTORY` so encounter victory does not erase the witness decision. No choice means no side-quest flag or extra callback.
- **Canon and limits:** the named Wayfarer, the answering voice, and this report are proposed adaptation. The source does not identify the sound or establish that it belongs to the road stalker. Keep the witness unnamed, use sensory details only, and do not explain or confirm the voice's nature.
- **Cast and staging:** Scout Tessa remains the road guide; the Lost Wayfarer has a distinct goal of warning travellers while staying anonymous; Courier Pell carries the record later. The scene reuses its existing cast placements, art, and connected searchmap.
- **Failure and scope:** the player may leave without speaking to the Wayfarer; the campaign remains completable. There is no reward, combat bonus, or encounter gate. A failed or skipped conversation must not fabricate a testimony. Isolated GemRB Xvfb sessions displayed the Wayfarer scene and both response controls; selecting the anonymous-warning choice wrote the expected Act II Journal entry. Direct Watchpost scene checks with each reporting choice confirmed that Pell honors privacy and leaves the voice's source unknown; the anonymous branch also acknowledged the cleared road and rubbing. Campaign-flow checks cover all four combinations of reporting choice and ambush state. These Watchpost checks initialize the relevant flags directly; a continuous transition from Mistwood and save/load still need live verification. An initial capture inherited the desktop display and was blank; rerunning under Xvfb fixed the capture.

## Delivery order

- **Slice A: Present characters in all current scenes.** Add named actors and a local Talk interaction with reusable dialogue data. Verify actors spawn once on initial entry and area transitions.
- **Slice B: Quest and event loop.** Add branching response options, quest flags, journal updates, event triggers, rewards, and battle gates. Each choice must affect a later beat or route.
- **Slice C: Scene fidelity.** Replace shared demo walkmesh, doors, entrances, and tutorial remnants with area-specific collision maps and transitions. Test movement and clicks in all areas.
- **Slice D: Campaign depth.** Expand each act into several connected subareas with side quests and recurring companions, preserving the user's Terralum chronology.
- **Slice E: Production review.** Play the entire campaign from a fresh save, test repeated area entry and save/load, verify Kokoro narration and ambient loops, then commit, comment, and push.

The campaign now has twenty-five scene areas and 63 story beats. Act II now includes a Mistwood Waywarden Relay after the Ruined Watchpost. Keeper Orla asks whether Algoreth calls the west patrol or keeps the signal dark; Waywarden Saren responds according to whether the patrol schedule was copied, and the selected signal is journaled. The relay has generated concept art and a connected custom searchmap. All 25 scene areas now have custom searchmaps, with connectivity checks for party starts, NPCs, encounter spawns, exits, and blocked props; live movement and the shared WED geometry remain open production gaps. Act III returns to the Mistwood Watchpost after Lorian. Pell lets Algoreth choose between a public route marker and a crown-held patrol map; the journaled choice changes Alisande’s court response. The return uses the existing location art and searchmap under a new area resource and cast. Act IV includes Captain Rovan’s journaled choice to keep the western road open to supervised scholars and travellers or restrict scroll access until the royal pair hears a clear purpose. Royal Scribe Oren then lets the player determine whether the route testimony and scroll account sit side by side or remain separate. Both decisions are journaled, and Queen Alisande’s later response reflects Rovan’s policy, Pell’s bounded testimony, and Oren’s filing choice. Act V adds a Containment Annex between the academy courtyard and vault. Warden Celene asks Algoreth to choose a measured boundary or an explicit containment purpose; the choice is journaled, changes Magister Vael's vault dialogue, and alters the sealing narration while preserving the established contained-echo outcome. The addition uses established characters and a proposed procedure, not a new explanation of the Tear. Act VII now continues from the supported departure with an adaptation arrival at Eldrador's Landing Quay, where Vaelthira offers a choice between witness testimony and measured seal records, followed by a Lower Sanctuary Threshold scene where the party chooses its approach to the sealed gate. The evidence choice changes Vaelthira's response at the threshold; both decisions are journaled. The campaign stops before entering or resolving the Tear. Both scenes have generated concept art, custom connected searchmaps, and named local casts. The Act VI optional scholar quest in the Deep Archives allows Archivist Nera to leave the Index Warden undisturbed or call it to account for a missing comparison leaf; victory records the recovered leaf, while leaving the folio sealed changes Selene’s and Ealdrin’s Index Hall dialogue. Selene identifies the source as a titleless living record whose speaker is unknown. If the player keeps the account sealed, a required two-stage dialogue chooses which bounded question Ealdrin carries to Eldrador; Queen Vaelthira’s welcome changes accordingly. Earlier Act I and II preparations and the lore boundaries remain documented in `lore_notes.md` and `campaign_plan.md`; preserve those provenance labels as new scenes are added.
